# Skills Restructure Proposal -- AID around six phases and one front door

Analysis input for this work. It takes the binding decisions in this work's `REQUIREMENTS.md`
(§ 1 Objective, § 2 Problem, § 5 FR-1..FR-3, § 6 NFR-Q) as fixed and proposes how every skill,
agent and artifact of AID is reorganized to satisfy them. Every count below was measured from
disk in this worktree. Citations are a path plus a heading or symbol.

### 1.4 Structural problems, with evidence

**P1 -- The skill surface is a parameter grid, not a set of jobs.** 68 of 112 skills are bindings
(§ 1.1). The behaviour lives in four shared bodies -- `shortcut-engine.md`,
`canonical/aid/templates/design-lifecycle.md`, `canonical/skills/aid-create-document/SKILL.md`,
`canonical/skills/aid-test/SKILL.md` -- and the per-artifact knowledge already lives in data:
`canonical/aid/templates/shortcut-scaffolding/<family>.md` ("CAPTURE -- minimal slot list, per
artifact", "DETAIL -- default task breakdown, per artifact"). The directories add names, not
behaviour.

**P2 -- Hosts cannot route 112 skills.** `.aid/design/skill-routing.md` § Problem records that in a
live Claude Code session `aid-execute`, `aid-plan`, `aid-detail` "appeared as bare names with no
description" because the skill listing overflowed its budget. A plain-language request then has
only a name to match.

**P3 -- Confusable names.** Beyond describe/define/specify/plan/detail (named in this work's
§ 2), one subject spawns many near-names: `aid-design-architecture`, `aid-create-architecture`,
`aid-update-architecture`, `aid-document-architecture`; `aid-test`, `aid-create-test`,
`aid-update-test`, `aid-design-test`, `aid-design-testing-strategy`; `aid-update`,
`aid-update-kb`, `aid-update-document`, `aid-update-ticket`. The catalog keeps a vestigial
`alias_of` field with three consumer classes blocking its removal
(`shortcut-catalog.yml`, field contract `alias_of`).

**P4 -- Routing exists, but only as advice, and allocation is duplicated.** `/aid-triage` is
"suggest-only" and "never invokes another skill on the user's behalf"
(`canonical/skills/aid-triage/SKILL.md` § State: HALT). The new-vs-continuation decision is
re-implemented at every starter: `canonical/aid/templates/work-initiation-gate.md` lists the engine
plus nine skills under "Consulted by", `design-lifecycle.md` § Skill shape binds 36 more to it,
and six downstream skills each repeat locate-and-enter via
`canonical/aid/templates/downstream-worktree-entry.md`. `aid-describe/SKILL.md` § Task Routing
restates the gate inline at length.

**P5 -- Two work layouts and three levels of state.** `work-state-template.yml` carries a
"Full-layout omission rule" listing keys that exist only on one layout; `pipeline.path: lite | full`
selects the layout; `aid-execute/SKILL.md` § Check 1 step 4 ("Detect the layout") branches every
read. A delivery has its own `STATE.yml`, a task has its own `STATE.yml` on the full path but lives
in `tasks_lifecycle` on the Lite path. `dashboard/reader/parsers.py` and siblings parse both.

**P6 -- "Feature" is an engineering unit, and it over-splits.** `aid-define` FEATURE-DECOMPOSITION
"decompose Functional Requirements (§5) into discrete, independently implementable features"
(`canonical/skills/aid-define/references/state-feature-decomposition.md`), and its process requires
that "Every §5 functional requirement maps to at least one feature"
(`canonical/skills/aid-define/references/feature-decomposition.md` § Step 2: Propose Feature List). That is a
work-breakdown unit, not a business capability (FR-3). Each feature then carries its own
`#### Technical Specification` and a `features[]` state row (`work-state-template.yml` key
`features`).

**P7 -- Deliveries and waves are authored structure over derivable data.** `aid-plan` writes
delivery stanzas plus per-delivery `STATE.yml`; `aid-execute` § Check 5 creates **one branch per
delivery** (`aid/{work}-delivery-NNN`) inside a work that already has its own worktree branch
(`work-initiation-gate.md` § 3a step 2). `canonical/aid/scripts/execute/derive-waves.sh` exists
because a wave-map "carries no information the table does not already carry". D29 already removed
`PLAN.md` from the Lite path for the same reason (`.aid/knowledge/decisions.md` § D29).

**P8 -- Too many KB writers.** At least 19 skills write under `.aid/knowledge/`: `aid-discover`
(plus its "Targeted Discovery (Re-entry)"), `aid-update-kb`, `aid-housekeep` (KB-DELTA and the
Conformance Lane), `aid-describe` DESCRIBE-SEED (greenfield), the 14 `aid-create-*`/`aid-update-*`
KB/product-doc skills, and `aid-summarize` (`kb.html`). FR-2 names four operations.

**P9 -- Human review is whole-document.** The shortcut engine's terminal state lists file names and
says "Review the documents above" (`shortcut-engine.md` § State: APPROVAL-HALT, Step 1);
`aid-describe` ends "by presenting the finished requirements for your approval" (frontmatter
description). On the full path a user approves Describe, Define, each Specify section, Plan, Detail
and every delivery gate -- each on the full artifact. Nothing ranks decisions by importance or
batches trivial ones.

**P10 -- Accumulated drift shows the structure is past its maintainable size.**
- `canonical/aid/rules.md` § Workflow lists seven phases; `.aid/knowledge/architecture.md`
  § Process Architecture is titled "The Six-Phase Pipeline" (2a/2b).
- `docs/aid-methodology.md` § Three Doors In says "The catalog's other 24 rows are hand-authored";
  the catalog has 60.
- `.aid/knowledge/pipeline-contracts.md` § Per-Skill State Machines says "one invocation advances
  one state, then stops. No auto-advance", while every skill chains per
  `canonical/aid/templates/state-machine-chaining.md` § 1. CHAIN.
- At least four `SKILL.md` files (`aid-ask`, `aid-define`, `aid-deploy`, `aid-describe`) still
  address work state as markdown `STATE.md` sections although it now lives in `STATE.yml` (e.g. `aid-define/SKILL.md` "Precondition: `## Interview State: Approved` must be
  present in `.aid/works/{work}/STATE.md`").
- `canonical/aid/templates/task-detail-template.md` still has `**Source:** feature-NNN-{name} ->
  delivery-NNN -> AC-N` and "that feature SPEC", both retired by D29.
- `aid-describe/SKILL.md` § Agents Involved cites "The frontmatter default `agent:
  aid-interviewer`", but no skill frontmatter carries an `agent:` key.
- `design-lifecycle.md` § The three stages needs a note titled "This 22 is not REQUIREMENTS FR-11
  CC-7's 22." to keep two coincidentally equal populations apart.

**P11 -- Two agents have no real job.** `aid-orchestrator` is dispatched only by `aid-monitor`'s
ROUTE state (`canonical/skills/aid-monitor/SKILL.md` § Dispatch). `aid-interviewer` is never
dispatched at all: the interview runs inline, and `aid-define`'s references dispatch other agents
"overriding the default `aid-interviewer`" (`state-feature-decomposition.md`). A sub-agent also
cannot hold a conversation with the user, which is what both roles describe.

## 2. Full skill inventory

Phase column uses the new model: Prep (Preparation), Idea (Ideation), Spec (Specification),
Plan (Planning), Build (Building), CI; "KB" for the four KB operations; "Orch" for the
orchestrator; "--" for out-of-phase utilities. Target names are defined in § 4.

Rows marked (fork) are genuinely debatable; § 7 names the fork. Verdicts: **Keep** (same job, maybe re-scoped), **Rename**, **Merge into X** (becomes a mode or a
parameter of X), **Absorb into X** (X takes over the behaviour; the skill disappears),
**Make internal** (stays a skill, not advertised), **Retire**.

## 4. Proposed organization

### 4.1 Layers

```
rules.md "Front door" clause  (always loaded, every host)
        |
        v
/aid  -- the orchestrator skill (main thread: intent, state, route, conversation, approvals)
        |
        +-- phase skills (one job each, user-invocable for power users)
        |     aid-kb-build -> aid-ideate -> aid-specify -> aid-plan -> aid-build -> aid-ci
        |
        +-- internal capabilities (called by /aid or by phase skills)
        |     aid-kb-query  aid-kb-update  aid-kb-view  aid-research  aid-prototype
        |     aid-review    aid-test       aid-ticket   aid-release   aid-monitor
        |
        +-- shared data and protocols (no skill directories)
              intent-catalog.yml + artifact-profiles/   conversation-protocol.md
              work-state template (one layout)          grade.sh, derive-waves.sh, ...
        |
        v
agents: architect, developer, reviewer, researcher, tech-writer, operator, clerk
```

### 4.2 The orchestrator (`/aid`)

**What it is.** A skill, `canonical/skills/aid/SKILL.md`, that runs in the main conversation and
owns five things no other skill owns: intent, state, route, conversation, approvals. It never
authors a phase artifact itself; it reads and follows the phase skill's body (the same
"execute that SKILL.md exactly as written" mechanism the kind-siblings use today, e.g.
`aid-document-runbook/SKILL.md`), so it is host-agnostic.

**Where it lives -- both places, with a strict split.**
- `canonical/aid/rules.md` gains a short `## Front door` section (target: under ten lines): any
  request to build, change, fix, discuss, review, release or ask about this project goes through
  the `aid` skill; an explicit `/aid-<skill>` always wins; questions about the project are answered
  through the KB. This is what makes a plain request reach `/aid` in hosts whose own description
  matching is unreliable (`.aid/design/skill-routing.md` § Problem). The rules hold the trigger only,
  never the logic, because they load in every session.
- The skill holds the logic, split into references: `intent.md` (classification),
  `state.md` (detection), `routes.md` (route table, generated from the intent catalog),
  `approvals.md`, plus the shared `conversation-protocol.md`.

**It is the sole work allocator.** It absorbs `work-initiation-gate.md` (enumerate works across
worktrees, new vs continuation, create and enter the worktree) and `downstream-worktree-entry.md`
(locate and enter). Phase skills receive a resolved work; invoked directly without one, they hand
intake back to `/aid`. That removes the gate restatements across the starters listed in P4.

**Intent detection.** One classification turn over the user's words plus any referenced input,
using the intent catalog. Classes:

| Intent class | Signals | Route |
|---|---|---|
| question | interrogative about the project | `aid-kb-query` (falls back to `aid-research` on a gap); no work |
| continue | "continue", "where are we", a work id, or cwd inside a `work-NNN` worktree | resume from `STATE.yml` |
| idea / discuss | "discuss", "what if", "I'm thinking about", no concrete artifact | depth `full` from Ideation |
| build a capability | "add", "build", "support", or a ticket reference | richness check decides `fast` or `full` |
| change on a known artifact | verb + artifact from the catalog (create/change/fix/refactor/remove/deprecate/migrate/test/experiment) | depth `fast` |
| document | "write/update a <genre>" | depth `direct` |
| design only | "design the X", "sketch the X" | Ideation with the artifact profile; stops at the seed |
| investigate | "should we", "compare", "analyze the data" | `aid-research`; may feed Ideation |
| try | "prototype", "quick mock" | `aid-prototype` |
| review / verify only | "review my PR", "run a security scan" | `aid-review` / `aid-test`; findings may open a `fix` work |
| KB fact change | "we now use Postgres", "that module is gone" | `aid-kb-update` (targeted) |
| ship / observe | "release", "check production" | `aid-release` / `aid-monitor` |
| meta | settings, connectors, chat | `aid-config` / `aid-connector` / `aid-chat` |

When two classes fit, the orchestrator asks one NFR-Q question with its recommendation; it never
guesses silently and never shows a menu of skills.

**State detection.** Read from disk every time, never from memory:
1. `.aid/` present? If not, bootstrap through `aid-config`.
2. KB present and approved? If not, Preparation is owed (brownfield: `aid-kb-build discover`;
   greenfield: deferred, see § 4.6). KB stale (`approved_at_commit` behind HEAD on documented
   paths)? Note it; queue an `aid-kb-update sweep` at the next natural pause, not before the user's
   request.
3. Active work: the current worktree's `work-NNN` branch, else `enumerate-works.sh`. If works exist
   and the request does not name one, ask new-vs-continuation (the gate's question, kept).
4. For the active work, `STATE.yml` `phase`, `lifecycle`, open `decisions`, task states -> the
   next step.

**Route selection: depth, not a different pipeline.** Every work runs the same six phases and
leaves the same artifacts; what changes is how many of them the user is asked to review. This keeps
the engine's property that it "never skips a phase, it collapses the information capture within
each phase" (`shortcut-engine.md`, opening paragraph) and drops the two-layout split.

| Depth | Used for | What the user sees before Build | Later checkpoints |
|---|---|---|---|
| `full` | thin input, ideas, broad capabilities | Ideation conversation and seed recap; PRD review in chunks; plan digest | Build check-ins at stage ends; CI merge approval |
| `fast` | rich input (ticket/description passes the richness check), known artifact changes, fixes, refactors | one combined review: important decisions first, then the plan digest; trivial decisions batched | CI merge approval |
| `direct` | documents, diagrams, other cheap-to-review outputs | nothing; Spec and Plan are generated and recorded | review of the result itself, then placement/merge |

**Richness check (rich vs thin).** A fixed checklist scored against the input (ticket body via
`aid-ticket read`, or the user's message): objective stated, user/actor known, scope bounded,
acceptance criteria present or derivable, constraints known. All present -> `fast`; gaps limited to
Low-importance items -> `fast` with those gaps decided by the AI and batched for confirmation; any
High-importance gap -> start the conversation at that gap (Ideation or Specification, whichever
owns it). The orchestrator says what it concluded ("this ticket is complete enough to go straight
to a plan") as a recommendation the user can override.

**Escalation and de-escalation.** A `fast` or `direct` route escalates when a real fork of High
importance appears, when the generated plan exceeds a size threshold (a settings key, default to
be chosen), or when a gate fails twice. A `full` route de-escalates when the user says "just do it":
remaining decisions are taken by the AI and batched. Every switch is announced in one line and
recorded in `STATE.yml`.

**Chaining.** Phase transitions chain inside one conversation. The orchestrator stops only at the
approvals of the route, at the rest points of the conversation protocol, and on an impediment.
Resuming in a new session is the "continue" intent.

**Relation to existing pieces.**
- `aid-triage`: its CLASSIFY logic (`references/state-classify.md`) is the seed of `intent.md`. The
  difference is the one this work names: `/aid` acts and keeps going; triage only suggested.
- `.aid/design/skill-routing.md`: superseded. With 20 skills there is no listing overflow, and the
  proposed routing table collapses to one row (everything goes to `/aid`). Its option 2
  (description hygiene) still applies to the 20 survivors. Delete the seed when this work ships,
  as the seed itself instructs.
- `aid-orchestrator` agent: its Feedback Routing table (Q&A, IMPEDIMENT, monitor findings) moves
  into `/aid`'s `routes.md`.

## 5. Artifact model per phase

### 5.1 The rule that applies to every phase

Each phase keeps **one agent-facing artifact** -- complete, guard-railed, graded -- and gets a
**human-facing view generated from it on demand**, never stored as a second document (a stored
digest would drift from its source). The view follows one protocol,
`canonical/aid/templates/conversation-protocol.md`, which absorbs the NFR-7 envelope
(`aid-describe/references/advisor-stance.md` § NFR-7 Question-Envelope Contract) and adds:

1. **Extract decisions** from the artifact: every choice the AI made or needs.
2. **Rate importance**: High (changes scope, user-visible behaviour, data, security, cost, or is
   hard to reverse), Medium (shapes implementation; moderate rework), Trivial (naming, internal
   structure, reversible cheaply).
3. **Order and chunk**: High first; one topic per turn; related decisions grouped into one chunk;
   each question states its importance.
4. **Ask per NFR-Q**: always a recommendation; a second option only for a real fork or equal
   validity; never a bad option; always a free-form answer.
5. **Rest points**: after each chunk, offer to stop; the queue persists in `STATE.yml`, so resuming
   in a new session continues at the next open decision.
6. **Trivial batch**: the AI decides trivial items; they are shown together at the end for one
   confirmation ("change any?").

Decisions live in one queue, `STATE.yml` `decisions:` (id, phase, topic, importance, status
`open | answered | decided-by-ai | confirmed`, recommendation, answer, applied-to). It replaces
the work `qa:` list, the "Cross-phase Q&A" entries, and `review_history`. KB-scoped questions stay
in `.aid/knowledge/STATE.md`, in the same entry shape.

## 7. Open forks for the maintainer

Ordered by importance. Each has a recommendation; the alternatives listed are valid ones.

### High

**F1 -- Mandatory human approvals per depth.** The decisions say the user stays in control
"through the final approvals" but do not fix which.
*Recommendation:* every work that changes code has two mandatory approvals -- the plan of record
before Building (PRD plus task digest, combined on `fast`) and the merge at the end of CI; `full`
adds seed and PRD approvals; `direct` keeps only the approval of the result. *Why:* one approval
before cost is spent and one before the change lands is the smallest set that keeps control; more
reproduces P9.
*Alternative:* allow `fast` routes below a size threshold to skip the pre-Build approval and rely
on the merge approval alone.

**F2 -- Deploy and Monitor placement.**
*Recommendation:* CI ends at an approved, merge-ready (or merged) result; `aid-release` is an
optional internal capability after CI; `aid-monitor` is an intake source that opens new works.
*Why:* the community CI phase is about preparing a merge; releasing and observing production act on
merged history across many works, not on one work.
*Alternative:* fold release into CI as an optional last step for projects that release per merge
(continuous deployment), keeping `aid-monitor` as intake.

**F3 -- Should the front door engage on every plain-language request?**
*Recommendation:* yes -- the rules.md clause routes every build/change/fix/discuss/review/release
request to `/aid`, which picks `direct` for trivial work so the cost of the process stays near
zero. *Why:* the leading goal is transparent skill use; an opt-in front door returns the user to
choosing.
*Alternative:* engage only when the user mentions AID or invokes `/aid`, leaving ordinary chat
untouched.

**F4 -- Compatibility for today's slash names.**
*Recommendation:* clean break in a major version, with a one-page old-to-new table (§ 6) in the
release notes. *Why:* D23 set the clean-switch precedent; the catalog deliberately has no redirect
mechanism, and roughly 90 stub directories would recreate P2 for a release.
*Alternative:* ship thin stubs for the 18 most-used old names for one minor version, each printing
"use /aid" and exiting.

### Medium

**F5 -- Where the Ideation seed lives.**
*Recommendation:* `.aid/design/<slug>.md` while the idea is being shaped, consumed (deleted) when
its PRD is approved -- the rule `design-lifecycle.md` already applies to `create`. *Why:* an idea
must survive before a work exists and across sessions; work folders are transient by rule.
*Alternative:* `SEED.md` inside the work folder, allocating the work at the first Ideation turn.

**F6 -- Greenfield KB timing.**
*Recommendation:* for a project with no code, Preparation records only the stack and conventions
the user states; `aid-kb-build` mode `forward` writes architecture and decisions right after the
PRD is approved. *Why:* today's DESCRIBE-SEED forward-authors architecture before any technical
decision is made; after Specification the decisions exist.
*Alternative:* keep forward authoring at the end of Ideation, as today.

**F7 -- Rename `REQUIREMENTS.md` to `PRD.md`.**
*Recommendation:* rename. *Why:* the phase model names the artifact a PRD, and the structure
changes anyway (nested ACs, one technical approach, optional features).
*Alternative:* keep the filename and change only the structure, which spares the dashboard,
`slice-requirements.sh` and the `work-artifact` criteria (`authoring-conventions.md` G-14/G-15)
one rename.

**F8 -- Phase skill names.**
*Recommendation:* `aid-ideate`, `aid-specify`, `aid-plan`, `aid-build`, `aid-ci`, with
`aid-kb-build` as the Preparation entry. *Why:* they mirror the six community phase names users
learn, and describe/define/detail are gone.
*Alternative:* artifact names -- `aid-seed`, `aid-prd`, `aid-tasks`, `aid-build`, `aid-ci` --
which avoid giving `aid-specify` and `aid-plan` new meanings.

**F9 -- Milestones in place of deliveries.**
*Recommendation:* an optional `group:` label on tasks, one branch per work, one build gate per work.
*Why:* deliveries add a state level, a branch level and a gate per group (P7) for information the
dependencies already carry.
*Alternative:* for large works, a milestone may become its own PR and CI pass, so a long work can
merge incrementally.

### Low

**F10 -- Merge `aid-tech-writer` into `aid-developer`.**
*Recommendation:* keep it. *Why:* user-facing prose is a different skill from code, and agents are
invisible to users, so merging buys little against problem 3.
*Alternative:* merge into one builder agent that executes every task type.

**F11 -- Product-planning documents (roadmap, backlog, MVP).**
*Recommendation:* keep them as KB document kinds handled by `aid-kb-update` mode `doc` and
ideated through `aid-ideate` profiles; D28 still holds. *Why:* they inform the orchestrator's
intake ("work on the next backlog item") at no extra skill cost.
*Alternative:* drop them from AID's scope and leave planning documents to the team's tracker.

**F12 -- Hiding internal skills from the host's command menu.**
*Recommendation:* keep all 20 invocable and mark internal ones in their description only. *Why:*
20 skills fit every host's listing; hiding is host-specific and blocks power users.
*Alternative:* render host-specific "not user-invocable" flags for the ten internal skills where
the host supports them.
